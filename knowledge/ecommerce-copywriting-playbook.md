# Ecommerce Copywriting Playbook

Use this supplementary playbook for ecommerce product, collection, and promotion copy. It distils the user-supplied ecommerce copywriting PDF into reusable checks. It is not a product-fact source and cannot override the current brief, LP, product page, visible creative, brand profile, platform constraint, or claim-safety rule.

## The job of ecommerce copy

Copy makes a product easier to understand and choose. It should connect a supported product detail to a shopper's situation, remaining hesitation, and next action. It is not a literary exercise and it is not a substitute for product evidence.

## Required product-to-shopper bridge

Before drafting, complete this chain:

```text
named shopper problem → use moment → source-backed product help → available proof → remaining objection → next action
```

If any required link is missing, ask for the source or mark the draft as an evidence-limited candidate. Never fill a missing link with unverified performance, social proof, scarcity, or urgency.

## Choose the right argument

Use the funnel stage and evidence available, not a one-size-fits-all formula.

| Situation | Prefer | Do not assume |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cold shopper | recognisable problem, use scene, clear product help | the shopper knows the brand or offer |
| Comparing options | selection logic, plain-language explanation, source-backed difference | technical terms are persuasive by themselves |
| Returning or ready to buy | a verified offer, hesitation answer, practical reason to act | a deadline, stock level, or guarantee that is not sourced |
| Mature product with verified customer evidence | verified review, outcome, or use story | testimonials or performance numbers without a source |
| New product with limited proof | product explanation, use case, and transparent selection logic | borrowed reputation or invented user experience |

## Field jobs

### Hook or headline

Do one job: open with a shopper question, a recognisable use scene, or a clear supported help. The hook must earn attention without merely restating text already visible on the creative.

### Body copy

Explain the product in plain language, surface the relevant doubt, and show why the supported feature matters in the stated moment. A technical term may stay only when it is translated into its useful outcome.

### Proof

Use only current evidence: product page details, supplied creative, verified review, verified campaign terms, or an approved source. Explain what the proof means for the shopper; do not list a badge, number, or term without context.

### CTA

Name a useful next step: view the product, compare a collection, check a size guide, or use a verified offer. The CTA should match the funnel stage and reduce the next decision, rather than only say “buy now.”

## Language checks

- Replace vague adjectives with a supported action, detail, time, result, or concrete scene.
- Do not use jargon as a shortcut for explanation.
- Do not use a story, testimonial, result number, or endorsement unless it is source-backed.
- Keep the product, audience, and reason to click identifiable. If the copy could fit any category after changing the product name, revise it.
- The universal anti-AI rules and brand-specific guardrails remain hard constraints.

## Editorial preflight

Before the normal quality gate, check:

1. Is the shopper problem specific enough to guide the hook?
2. Does the product feature appear in a believable use moment?
3. Is the practical help clear without relying on jargon or empty adjectives?
4. Is every proof claim traceable to the current source?
5. Does the CTA tell the shopper what to do next and why that route helps?

Any “no” requires revision or a `blocked` evidence state.
